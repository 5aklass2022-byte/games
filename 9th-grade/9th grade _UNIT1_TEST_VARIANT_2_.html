I. Reading · 10 points

Students Who Make a Difference

At the beginning of the school year, eighteen-year-old Anna decided to become a member of her school student council. She wanted students to have more opportunities to share their opinions and improve school life. During the first meeting, the council discussed three problems: too much plastic waste, a lack of quiet study areas, and limited activities after lessons. Anna suggested working with local organisations instead of trying to solve every problem alone. The council invited a recycling group to organise a workshop and asked the head teacher for permission to create a reading corner. While some students collected books, others designed posters to explain the project. The council also prepared a survey so that classmates could choose future activities. Not everyone agreed with the proposals, but the members listened carefully and made changes. After six weeks, the school had a small reading corner and several new recycling bins. Anna was pleased because students had taken responsibility for their environment. She understood that leadership does not mean giving orders. It means encouraging people, respecting different opinions, and finding practical solutions together. Next year, she plans to study social sciences and continue volunteering in her community.
